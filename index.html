Part I. Obstructive Lung Disease

COPD with Emphysema

Read / download the COPD emphysema article (PDF)

In emphysema, destruction of alveolar walls raises lung compliance, causes expiratory flow limitation and air trapping, and creates intrinsic PEEP. Most stable patients keep a near-normal PaCO₂ through a high neural drive; some become chronically hypercapnic when the load on a hyperinflated, shortened diaphragm exceeds its strength. This article builds normocapnic and hypercapnic emphysema patients in VentiVerse from published measurements (FEV₁, lung volumes, blood gases, bicarbonate, inspiratory strength, neural drive).

Simulations of low-flow nasal oxygen, nasal high flow, CPAP, pressure support, and nocturnal NIV are compared with published studies (Robinson 2000, Markovitz 2010, McKinstry 2018, Fraser 2016, O'Donoghue 2002, Vitacca 2004, Murphy 2012, Köhnlein 2014), with a summary of agreement across all comparisons and a treatment strategy for the chronic patient.

COPD with Chronic Bronchitis

Read / download the chronic bronchitis article (PDF)

Stable COPD with chronic bronchitis combines airway narrowing and mucus with low ventilation–perfusion units, chronic hypercapnia, and hypoxemia that is severe for the degree of airflow obstruction. This article builds a chronic bronchitis patient in VentiVerse and simulates 8-hour wake and sleep periods on room air, nasal oxygen, bronchodilators, CPAP and bilevel pressure support.

Simulated values are compared with published physiological studies, together with a literature-based chart of FEV₁ loss and COPD admission risk with mucus hypersecretion (Vestbo 1996, Allinson 2016).

Acute Exacerbation of COPD

Read / download the COPD exacerbation article (PDF)

Acute exacerbation of COPD is treated along a ladder of respiratory support: controlled oxygen, high-flow nasal therapy, noninvasive ventilation and invasive ventilation, on a background of bronchodilators and corticosteroids with physiological drug timing. This article describes how the VentiVerse exacerbation model represents the patient and how each therapy acts on it.

Simulated responses are compared with published studies and trials, including Brochard 1990 and Aubier 1980, with the early-treatment course and recovery over weeks (Seemungal 2000, Parker 2005).

COPD–Obstructive Sleep Apnea Overlap Syndrome

Read / download the COPD/OSA overlap article (PDF)

The overlap syndrome produces more nocturnal desaturation, daytime hypercapnia and pulmonary hypertension than either disease alone, and higher mortality when untreated. This article adds a bounded Starling-resistor pharynx, arousal and a lung oxygen-store model to the VentiVerse COPD lung, and simulates COPD alone, OSA alone and overlap through stochastic nights.

Oxygen, nasal high flow, CPAP titration and bilevel therapy are compared with published cohorts and trials, with published survival and outcome data (Chaouat 1995, Marin 2010, Machado 2010).

Bronchiectasis with Chronic Hypercapnic Respiratory Failure

Read / download the bronchiectasis article (PDF)

Advanced bronchiectasis causes chronic hypoxemic and then hypercapnic respiratory failure, treated with long-term oxygen and nocturnal noninvasive ventilation. This article builds a VentiVerse bronchiectasis patient matched to a published home-NIV cohort and simulates wake and sleep on room air, nasal oxygen, nasal high flow and bilevel ventilation.

Simulated nocturnal gas exchange and treatment responses are compared with published bronchiectasis and cystic fibrosis studies.

Status Asthmaticus

Read / download the status asthmaticus article (PDF)

Status asthmaticus that needs invasive ventilation is dominated by dynamic hyperinflation: the lungs cannot empty before the next breath. This article describes the VentiVerse intubated status asthmaticus patient and simulates changes in rate, minute ventilation, bronchodilators and external PEEP.

Plateau and peak pressures and PaCO₂ are compared with published ventilated cohorts (Leatherman 2004, Tuxen 1992), with a literature-based chart of progression from acute exacerbation to status asthmaticus.

Tracheal Stenosis

Read / download the tracheal stenosis article (PDF)

A fixed narrowing of the trachea loses pressure in proportion to the square of flow, so it can be silent at rest and still limit exercise and sleep. This article replaces the earlier resistance-only example with a VentiVerse orifice model set by the narrowest diameter, and simulates unsupported breathing from 18 to 4 mm, mild exercise, CPAP, bilevel ventilation and a tube placed past the lesion.

Pressure drops, blood gases before and after dilation, peak flow and exercise oxygenation are compared with published studies (Brouns 2007, Liang 2015, Demedts 1995, Al-Bazzaz 1975).

Tracheobronchomalacia

Read / download the tracheobronchomalacia article (PDF)

Tracheobronchomalacia (TBM) and excessive dynamic airway collapse (EDAC) narrow the central airway when pleural pressure exceeds luminal pressure, mainly on forced expiration and cough. This article replaces the earlier resistance-only example with a VentiVerse collapse model in which airway area depends on transmural pressure, and simulates collapse from 0 to 90%, quiet and active expiration, EDAC, TBM with COPD, CPAP titration, bilevel ventilation and a tube past the lesion. It is a standalone chapter, pages 371–390.

Dynamic CT collapse, CPAP needed to keep the airway open and blood gases are compared with published studies (Boiselle 2009, Baroni 2005, O'Donnell 2014, Funes-Ferrada 2024, Kandaswamy 2013).

Cystic Fibrosis with Chronic Hypercapnic Respiratory Failure

Read / download the cystic fibrosis article (PDF)

Chronic hypercapnia is a late complication of advanced cystic fibrosis lung disease. This article builds an adult VentiVerse example whose severity is set by FEV₁ (% predicted), with an elastic load that rises as FEV₁ falls, preserved muscle strength and a raised metabolic rate, and simulates wake and sleep on room air, oxygen, CPAP and bilevel ventilation.

Blood gases, breathing pattern, sleep studies and NIV responses are compared with published studies (Granton 1998, Hart 2002, Young 2008, Gozal 1997, Milross 2001, Hill 1998).

COPD with Expiratory Flow Limitation and Intrinsic PEEP

Read / download the expiratory flow limitation article (PDF)

In severe COPD, dynamic compression of narrow, poorly tethered airways chokes expiratory flow, traps gas and creates intrinsic PEEP. This article builds a VentiVerse example with a choke-point (waterfall) pressure and PEEP-dependent airway stenting, shows its Mead–Whittenberger loop, and simulates CPAP titration, bilevel ventilation and 8-hour wake and sleep periods.

Intrinsic PEEP, effort and lung-volume responses to external PEEP are compared with published studies (Appendini 1994 and 1996, O'Donoghue 2002, Petrof 1990, Gay 1989, Haluszka 1990), with a treatment strategy for setting CPAP or EPAP.

Bronchiolitis Obliterans Syndrome after Lung Transplantation

Read / download the bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome article (PDF)

A few lung transplant recipients with bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome develop chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure and are treated with home NIV. Published physiology is too sparse to calibrate a model, so this article describes an uncalibrated VentiVerse example: which parameters rest on measurements in transplant recipients, which must be supplied by the user, and an order for fitting them to a patient.

It gives physiology-based NIV suggestions that have not been tested against patient data. Inhaled bronchodilators are not indicated.

Part II. Neuromuscular Respiratory Failure

Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

Read / download the ALS article (PDF)

Respiratory failure is the main cause of death in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), and non-invasive ventilation (NIV), particularly when started early, prolongs survival. This article explains the respiratory physiology of ALS and a mathematical model of it implemented in the VentiVerse ventilatory physiology simulator. Clinic measurements (MIP, MEP, FVC % predicted, blood gases, demographics) set the respiratory muscle capacity, which is limited by length–tension, force–velocity and fatigue; the model also includes effort-driven rapid shallow breathing, ALS hypermetabolism, a bicarbonate-dependent CO₂ setpoint, sleep stages, and the NIV interface.

Bulbar- and limb-onset presets are demonstrated across wake and sleep, and simulations are compared one by one with published ALS case reports, with a discussion of where the model agrees and why it may differ. A dedicated section uses the cases and simulations to develop mechanical ventilation strategies.

Adult Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy and Chronic Neuromuscular Disease

Read / download the adult DMD article (PDF)

Respiratory failure is the main cause of death in Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) without ventilatory support, and nocturnal hypoventilation precedes daytime hypercapnia. Adults with DMD are small, weak, and have a stiff chest wall. This article builds an adult DMD model in VentiVerse from published cohort data (body size, strength, lung and chest-wall compliance, metabolism, bicarbonate) and a sleep model with REM-sleep hypopneas, a 90-minute NREM–REM cycle, and arousal from hypercapnia and hypoxemia.

Simulations are compared with published DMD reports and cohorts (Fanfulla 1998, Smith 1988 and 1989, Kim 2021, Segall 1988, Matsumura 1998, Beck 2006, Toussaint 2007). The cases and simulations support a strategy of treating the night first, always using a backup rate set a few breaths below the patient's own rate, sizing pressure support to the small, stiff DMD patient, choosing an inspiratory time of 30–40% of the cycle, and extending support into the day when daytime CO₂ rises.

Myasthenic Crisis

Read / download the myasthenic crisis article (PDF)

Myasthenic crisis is a failure of the respiratory pump rather than of the lungs. This article describes the VentiVerse myasthenia gravis model, with a pressure-capacity muscle model, use-dependent neuromuscular transmission fatigue, and simulated-time effects of neostigmine and pyridostigmine.

Crisis gases, BiPAP response, drug and plasma-exchange responses and unsupported breathing trials are compared with published reports (Agarwal 2006, Radwan 1988, Goti 1995, Borel 1993) and the 20/30/40 rule.

Guillain–Barré Syndrome

Read / download the Guillain–Barré syndrome article (PDF)

In Guillain–Barré syndrome, respiratory muscle weakness progresses over days while blood gases stay near normal until late. This article adds a days-since-onset clock to VentiVerse, with vital capacity falling to a nadir at about two weeks and recovering over months, and a correction for the upper airway bypassed by a breathing tube.

Unsupported breathing, NIV, ventilation and T-piece weaning trials are compared with published cohorts and cases (Lawn 2001, Chevrolet 1991, Borel 1993, Wijdicks, Pearse), using 8-hour simulations, comparison bars and identity charts.

Cervical Spinal Cord Injury

Read / download the spinal cord injury article (PDF)

After cervical spinal cord injury, breathing depends on the level of injury: diaphragm loss above C3, partial loss at C3–C5, and paralysis of the expiratory muscles at every cervical level. This article builds level-specific VentiVerse examples on a weeks-since-injury clock, with vital capacity recovering over the first year and higher lying down than sitting up.

Lung function, strength, need for ventilation, weaning time and an infection case are compared with published studies (Ledsome 1981, Como 2005, Mateus 2007, Linn 2001, Peterson 1999, Estenne 1987, An 2026).
